# Format: Old Wisdom, New Problems

- **Length:** 80–160 words
- **Goal:** Take a piece of old-fashioned advice (a saying, a rule people lived by, a grandparent-style lesson) and show it solves a very modern problem.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook: the old saying or rule, or a modern pain point that it answers (under 15 words).
  2. What people meant by it back then (2 sentences).
  3. The modern problem it fits: phones, burnout, money stress, comparison, relationships.
  4. A single standalone takeaway line.
  5. Close with a question inviting readers to share advice from their own elders.
- **Rule:** Do not attribute sayings to specific real people unless you are certain. "People used to say" is fine.
